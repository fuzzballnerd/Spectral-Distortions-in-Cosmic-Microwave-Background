## What's actually happening

AK19's own text (Sec. 4, describing Fig. 13) says explicitly why the real heat-fraction curve should flatten out above ~1 GeV: as the cascade develops, it produces "a progressively broader photon spectrum," and averaging over that spread of photon energies is what smooths out the boost/de-boost oscillation cycle at high energy.

Your code never produces that spread. In electron_cascade, each log-decade of the electron's cooling track is collapsed to a single characteristic IC photon energy:
E_gamma_char = (4.0/3.0) * gamma_e**2 * beta2 * E_cmb_char_eV
...

That single photon energy is then recursed — and at the next generation, it again collapses to one characteristic energy, and so on. Every generation is a delta-function stand-in for what should be a distribution. Since there's never any spread to average over, the "boost cycle" oscillation AK19 describes doesn't damp out with cascade depth — it persists and even amplifies through recursion. That's exactly the "very jagged/wavy, doesn't flatten" look in fig13_heat_fraction.png, and it's fully deterministic — not chaos.
